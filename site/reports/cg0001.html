CG-0001: UK SMS-phishing redirect chain on generated domains
Cluster CG-0001 in campaign-graph (36 hosts, density 0.959)
Status Unattributed. Active 21 Sep – 29 Oct 2026; landing pages suspended by 1 Nov 2026
Assessed activity SMS phishing (smishing) aimed at UK mobile users, through an affiliate-style click tracker
Collection Passive only: ThreatFox, RDAP registration records, certificate transparency, public reporting. No domain was resolved, visited or scanned
Analyst Barnabas · 1 November 2026

Key findings

  1. The cluster is a smishing redirect chain, not malware. ThreatFox labels all 46 records "Unknown malware / payload_delivery". The URLs show something else: a short generated domain, then a click tracker carrying an SMS cost macro (%sms_cost%) and a UK mobile number, then a landing page under /pa/UK/. High confidence.
  2. One operator built it in three registration waves (21–28 Sep, 13–18 Oct, 21–24 Oct), switching registrar and DNS setup each time. What links the waves is the naming: all 38 generated domains are 7–8 letters, alternating vowel and consonant (ihelaha, egulene, oramosi). The URL conventions and reporting times are shared too. Moderate confidence.
  3. The operator already held the victims' personal data before sending. One tracker URL carries a UK resident's first name, surname, home address, email and mobile number as pre-filled parameters. That points to a bought or breached lead list, not data typed into a phishing form. Moderate confidence, based on one complete example.
  4. Domains were aged before use. The median gap from registration to first report is 10 days (range 3–37). The oldest wave served only /opt/out pages. High confidence for the timing; low confidence for why.
  5. Partly disrupted already. Both .top landing domains are on registry serverHold. The 36 short domains and the tracker were still registered and unsuspended on 1 Nov 2026.

How campaign-graph found it

CG-0001 was the largest cluster with no family label. The graph joined its hosts on shared custom URI paths, /L12qt0W (35 hosts) and /opt/out (10 hosts), which count as strong pivots. No family label came from the feeds, so it surfaced in the explorer's Leads view.

Open CG-0001 in the explorer.

It is a fair test of the tool. Without labels, the cluster still matched a real operation, and the investigation below confirmed the grouping with evidence the graph never saw: registration records and naming.

The chain

SMS to a UK mobile
  └─ https://<generated>.com/L12qt0W           short link; the path is the click_id
       └─ https://now.trckthepage[.]com/<uuid>?click_id=L12qt0W&var2=…&var9=44…&cost=%sms_cost%
            └─ https://<generated>.top/pa/UK/<n>/?srv=eu&domain=now.trckthepage[.]com&cep=…&lptoken=…
                                               landing page (not visited)
  • The short-link path is the tracker's click_id. L12qt0W appears on 35 domains, so it is a batch or campaign token, not a per-recipient one. A second token, Fnc12IU, appears once.
  • The tracker's parameters are the generic slots ad trackers use (var2…var11, lptoken, cep). In the observed URLs, those slots carry the recipient's mobile number (var9, UK 44 7… format) and, in one case, name, address and email.
  • %sms_cost% is an unfilled cost macro, left in the URL by whatever system sent the SMS. It shows the traffic was bought or sent as SMS and tracked like paid ads.
  • /opt/out appears only on wave A domains. Fake opt-out links are common in smishing; one plausible use is confirming that a number is live when someone replies or clicks. This is unconfirmed, as no page content was collected.

Public reporting on UK smishing (Hunt.io: DPD-themed operation; Resecurity and Infosecurity Magazine on Smishing Triad) does not mention this tracker domain or these URL parameters. The tracker-macro design and the pre-filled victim data differ from the per-brand phishing kits in that reporting. No attribution is made.

Name generator. All 38 generated domains (tracker excluded) alternate vowel and consonant, are 7 or 8 letters long, and 37 of them start with a vowel. A pattern this narrow surviving three registrar changes is the strongest link between the waves.

Certificates. Wave A shows automated provisioning: 9 of its 10 domains got a Let's Encrypt wildcard certificate (*.domain) within an hour of registration. Wave B has 3 such certificates; 7 domains returned none and 1 lookup failed. In wave C, crt.sh returned no certificate for 14 domains and failed for 3; the one hit is iconado[.]com's older GoDaddy certificate. Missing certificates are a collection gap, not evidence of plain HTTP: the reported URLs use HTTPS.

Caveats

  • Single reporter. All 51 ThreatFox records (46 on cluster domains, 5 tracker and landing URLs) come from one reporter in two batches, so part of the cluster's coherence may reflect how one person submitted them. Registration and naming evidence was checked independently to counter this, and it holds.
  • Feed label is wrong. "Unknown malware / payload_delivery" describes no malware. This is phishing. A pipeline trusting the label would misfile it.
  • Nothing was visited. Lure text, landing-page brand and payment flow are unknown. Collecting them safely would mean a sandboxed browser from a UK mobile IP.
  • Victim data. One record holds a UK resident's personal data. It is redacted everywhere in this repository and in the published explorer. The original record stays visible on ThreatFox.

What would change this assessment

  • The lure text or landing brand (for example a parcel, toll or tax refund theme) would allow comparison with known kits and could support attribution.
  • Domains with the same name pattern registered at other registrars, or with other click_id values, would test H2 and size the operation.
  • Wave C certificates appearing in CT with the same issuer pattern as wave A would strengthen the single-operator link.

URL patterns

  • ^https://[a-z]{7,8}\.(com|top)/(L12qt0W|Fnc12IU|opt/out)$
  • tracker query containing click_id= and cost=%sms_cost%
  • landing path /pa/UK/<digits>/?srv=eu&domain=

Detection ideas

  • Mail and SMS gateways: flag newly registered 7–8 letter domains that alternate vowel and consonant, in messages to UK numbers. Expect false positives on real words; combine with domain age under 45 days.
  • Proxy and DNS logs: block or alert on the tracker domain, and on any URL holding an unfilled %…_cost% macro.
  • Brand protection: the /pa/<country>/ landing convention with srv= and lptoken= may recur for other countries; watch for /pa/IE/, /pa/US/.

Lessons fed back into campaign-graph

  • Feed URLs can hold victim personal data in query strings. The explorer now shows indicator URLs without query strings, and raw investigation data stays out of git.
  • Domain-name shape (this vowel–consonant generator) linked infrastructure the URL pivots alone did not. A nameshape: weak pivot is on the roadmap.
